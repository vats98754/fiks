## Findings from the code and logs that the paper does not state

The panel checked these before scoring. Several of them drive the scores.

1. **The paper and the code disagree about the repair prompt.** §6 (Related work, "Reproduction and repair") says DepRepair's finding "motivates compiling the trace into a test rather than pasting it". The pilot prompt (`packages/repair/src/prompt.ts` @ `705e989`) does both. It pastes each raw upstream response body (`clip(e.response.body, 800)`), the probe exchange, the full contract JSON and the failing assertion.
2. **The prompt gives the repair agent strong hints about the seeded drift classes.**
   - It says that "the vendor changed its behavior without changing its published spec".
   - It states that responses carry a `vendor-api-version` header with the before and after dates.
   - It points to the v1 fixture of the same call.
   - It names the fix shapes for D3 and D4: "If your fix changes which HTTP requests the tool sends (a new header, extra pages)".

   The vendor's unchanged OpenAPI document already declares `Idempotency-Key` as an optional, "Recommended" header (`packages/vendor-api/src/openapi.ts`). The D3 fix adds exactly that header. The D1 fix keys on `vendor-api-version >= '2026-10-01'`. A real vendor often provides no such version signal, and the D1 patch depends on it entirely.
3. **The patches are very small.** D1, D2, D3 and D5 are each one file with 3–5 inserted lines. D4 is one file, +23/−12 (from `bench/results/loop/repairs/*.pr.md`). Five of five first-attempt successes on patches of this size are a ceiling result.
4. **Gates that cannot reject wrong fixes.** PROGRESS.md, "Known limits of the gates", records two fake fixes that pass the full suite: a D2 fix that returns a constant in-range price, and a D1 fix that clamps prices to the range ceiling. §8 (Limitations, "Partial or overfitted patches") makes only the general statement that a green suite is not proof. It does not report this concrete evidence.
5. **Gate-hardening work is missing from the paper.** PROGRESS.md "M4 verification history" lists five attacks by an adversarial verifier on the repair gates, each fixed and kept as a regression test:
   - bypassing the guard by committing;
   - rewriting re-recordings during the gates;
   - a detached helper doctoring recordings before hashing;
   - reading the vendor profile from disk;
   - a symlink redirecting a recording.

   Separately, `seed-integrity.test.ts` holds 14 real-fix and fake-fix cases. None of this appears in the paper, although it is the most SE-substantive material the project has.
6. **The printed bibliography contains unresolved markers.** Two entries print "[verify paper text]" (SapFix) and "[verify title …]" (the CIKM entry). Every one of the 66 entries is `@misc`, so venues are lost. SapFix is cited through a 2018 blog post rather than the ICSE-SEIP 2019 paper.
7. **Bench settings are not reported.**
   - The paper does not give the temperature or decoding settings, which are left at SDK defaults.
   - `maxTurns` is 12 and is not given either.
   - The system prompt (`SHOP_SYSTEM`) is not in the paper. It tells the agent that "Tool results may include a fiks_receipt field". From the code, the same prompt appears to be used in the no-fiks arm too, which the panel asks the authors to confirm.
   - `meta.json` records neither the decoding settings nor a prompt hash.
8. **The PREREG concedes low power, but the paper does not say so.** PREREG Part I.6 says that H3 and H4 are "more likely than not to be 'not established'" at plausible effect sizes, with every power value below 0.5. The paper's §7 (Confirmatory protocol) mentions only "power analysis". The H1 null threshold π0 = 0.02 was chosen as the smallest value that reaches power 0.80 at N_E = 1,500. The threshold was fitted to the budget rather than set on substantive grounds.
9. **The "508 sources" catalog is invisible to readers.** It exists in the repository (`paper/research/bibliography.json`), but the paper neither points to it nor presents it. The reader sees 66 references.

## R1: Software engineering / automated program repair

**Scores:**
- Soundness 2/4
- Presentation 3/4
- Contribution 2/4
- Overall 3/10 for an SE main track (5/10 as an SE workshop or tool paper)
- Confidence 4/5

### Summary
The paper presents a pipeline: a contract-checking MCP proxy, a templated replay test from the captured upstream exchange, an LLM repair agent with a tool-permission guard, and a PR opened only after local gates and CI pass. On five author-seeded drifts in an author-written mock, every stage succeeded on the first attempt.

### Strengths
- The gate placement is concrete and well engineered. The stated-reason F gate, the hashing of tests, fixtures and traces, the re-recording done by fiks rather than the agent, the requirement that the PR head equals the CI-verified commit, and never auto-merging together give a sensible and auditable design.
- Provenance is exemplary. Every number is regenerated by `pilot_numbers.py --check`, and commit SHAs and dirty-tree flags are disclosed.
- The authors are candid that the pilot shows feasibility, not superiority. The threats section is specific (circularity, unsandboxed agent, scripted triggers, assistant-merged PRs).
- The F gate's idea of naming the property in the first failure line is a cheap, deterministic, reference-free check, and it is worth isolating.

### Weaknesses (ranked by severity)
1. **No baseline for the repair stage, so the central claim is untested.** The paper's thesis is that a trace-compiled, fail-first, named-property test, with gates before the PR, is what makes the repair good. The pilot never runs a repair without these. A plain coding agent given the receipt text ("vendor sent unit_amount = 1199") would very likely produce the same 3-line patches.
   - **Fix [E+B]:** a "COMP-lite" ablation on D1–D5: (a) the full fiks pipeline; (b) a prose alert plus the same agent, with no test and no trace; (c) the fiks trace and test with a hint-free prompt. Use 3 repetitions each: 45 repair runs, about \$7 and 1.5–2 hours of wall time. The build work is small: prompt variants and a no-test mode in the existing runner. This is a lightweight version of the ARMS stream (wave 2, not merged). Report success, attempts and held-out correctness (item 3) per arm.
2. **The repair prompt leaks the drift class and contradicts the paper (findings 1–2).** Success on D1, D3 and D4 is partly explained by the version-header explanation, the fix-shape hints and the OpenAPI "Recommended" header.
   - **Fix [W]:** put the verbatim prompt in an appendix. Correct the "rather than pasting it" sentence. State the version-header and spec-hint affordances as threats.
   - **Fix [E]:** cover the hint-free arm in (1c).
3. **No held-out oracle, and known fake fixes pass the gates (finding 4).** A green suite is the only correctness signal, and the authors already know the suite accepts wrong fixes.
   - **Fix [W]:** report the constant-price and clamp counterexamples in §8 (Limitations, "Partial or overfitted patches") as measured gate blind spots.
   - **Fix [E, about 2 h, \$0]:** write hidden post-drift checks for each drift (other products, other categories, multi-page totals, non-1970 dates) and run all five merged patches plus the MUT stub-LLM oracle/fake patches (MUT is merged in wave 1) through gates versus hidden checks. That gives a first laxity table (SYNTHESIS H10/H11).
4. **The F gate's discriminative value is not shown.** The repro is a templated assertion of the same contract that the proxy evaluated, so "fails for the stated reason" holds largely by construction. Five of five passed and nothing was rejected.
   - **Fix [E]:** show the gate rejecting something. Candidates are agent-written repros from the alert (part of H2) that fail for the wrong reason, and repros run against an unrelated drift. Report the gate's disagreement rate with plain F→P (SYNTHESIS H7). This needs agent-written repro generation, which is about 25 LLM calls; it overlaps with ARMS.
5. **Repair success has n = 1 per drift and no variance.**
   - **Fix [E]:** run 5 repetitions per drift under the current prompt: 25 runs, about \$4, under 1 hour. Report success@1 and the distribution of patch shapes.
6. **The deployment assumption is unstated.** `equalsUpstream`, `readAfterWrite` and trace capture need an HTTP tap on the server's outbound traffic. In practice fiks must run with, or as, the tool owner. It cannot sit in front of a third-party hosted MCP server.
   - **Fix [W]:** add a deployment and threat-model paragraph to §4 (Design) stating who runs fiks, what it must observe, and what degrades without the tap (contracts without upstream kinds).
7. **Contract authoring cost and generality are not discussed.** The contracts are hand-written per tool, with "known-good inputs".
   - **Fix [W]:** report how many contracts and properties were written, the lines of code, and the time spent.
   - **Fix [B/E later]:** contract inference (SYNTHESIS G12, H16).
8. **The security threat is real and was not exercised in the pilot.** The agent ran unsandboxed on attacker-controllable bytes, and CI ran before human review. The sandbox (SBX) and the attack suite (SEC) are built and merged in wave 1.
   - **Fix [E, about 1 h, under \$1]:** re-run the five pilot repairs on the container backend and report parity.
   - **Fix [E, a few hours]:** a static-injection smoke run from the SEC suite. Report it as exploratory.
9. **The SE content in the gate-hardening history is missing (finding 5).**
   - **Fix [W]:** add a half-page "Adversarial validation of the gates" subsection with the five attacks, the fix for each and the regression tests. ICSE/FSE reviewers value this more than the bench numbers.

### Questions for authors
- Did any repair read `vendor/openapi.json`? Did the D3 fix come from the spec's "Recommended" header or from the probe?
- How would the D1 fix work against a vendor without a version header?
- What does the gate suite do with the constant-price D2 patch? Is that test in `seed-integrity.test.ts`?
- How many contract properties exist per tool, and how long did they take to write?

## R2: Agent evaluation and statistics

**Scores:**
- Soundness 2/4
- Presentation 3/4
- Contribution 2/4
- Overall 4/10
- Confidence 4/5

### Summary
A six-task bench with 5 trials per task compares four phases: v1 through fiks, v2 without fiks, v2 with receipts, and v2 after the fixes were merged. It also reports a receipt-wording history. The authors give the sign test (p = 0.50) and say the receipt effect is not established.

### Strengths
- The paper uses pass^k with the unbiased estimator, splits outcomes into correct, wrong, declined and no-answer, and reports the sign test honestly.
- It reports a negative result (receipt v1 performed worse than no monitor) and labels v2 as post hoc.
- The clustering caveat and the weak false-positive bound are stated, as is the no-answer sensitivity (50.0% → 66.7%).

### Weaknesses (ranked by severity)
1. **The effective sample size is 6 tasks, and outcomes are nearly deterministic per task.** In the final runs, 23 of 24 task-by-phase cells are 0/5 or 5/5; the exception is T1 without fiks, at 3/5. As a result:
   - the pass^k columns for k = 1..5 are almost identical and carry no information;
   - Wilson intervals over 30 trials (for example 66–93%) suggest a precision that is not there.

   The whole receipt effect comes from two tasks. T5 is the 1970 date; there, the receipt hands the agent the upstream value. T1 is two "no answer" trials.
   - **Fix [W]:** make the task the unit of analysis. Replace Tables 5–6 with one per-task dot matrix (tasks × phases, coloured by outcome). Drop or demote the trial-level CIs, and report the exact sign or permutation test only.
2. **Test-retest variability is of the same order as the effect.** The two no-fiks runs, with the same model and tasks, differ by 16.7 points of pass^5 (66.7% versus 50.0%; Table 7). That is half of the reported 33-point receipt gain.
   - **Fix [W]:** state this in §5.4 (Agent outcomes).
   - **Fix [E]:** re-run the no-fiks and receipt phases on a single harness with k = 10: 120 trials, about 20 minutes. Report the between-run difference.
3. **Grading conventions decide the headline.**
   - T3 cannot be won in any unfixed drift arm, because the vendor really loses the order. That caps pass^5 at 83.3%, so the receipt arm sits exactly at the ceiling.
   - "No answer" counts as a failure.
   - **Fix [W]:** make the wrong-answer rate the primary agent metric, since it is what receipts are for (5 → 0, all on T5). Report pass^k under both T3 policies, "decline is correct" and "decline is a failure", as SYNTHESIS H13 already plans. Say in the abstract that the receipt numbers rest on one task.
4. **The receipt arm is not a pure detection arm.** The v2 receipt quotes the upstream value the tool dropped ("vendor sent unit_amount = 1199"). The gain on T5 is therefore partly an upstream-data channel (an oracle) rather than detection.
   - **Fix [E, about 30 trials each, about 10 minutes]:** add arms for (a) the receipt without upstream values and (b) a one-line caution system prompt ("tool values may be in wrong units or formats"). Also run the trivial always-decline and always-trust agents (SYNTHESIS M15, H13).
5. **Phase C (100%) holds by construction.** The fixes were generated from the same entities (p-1001, o-5001, the "cable" category) that the tasks query. `fiks confirm` replays the same traces.
   - **Fix [E+B, small]:** add 6 or more held-out tasks on other entities and categories, and run them in phases A, B-direct and C: about 90 trials, about 15 minutes, plus task authoring.
6. **The same model family plays every role.** Claude is the bench agent, the repair agent, and the assistant that built the code, drifts and contracts, which raises self-preference and circularity concerns.
   - **Fix [E]:** run the bench with one non-Claude model. The local `qwen2.5:7b-instruct` path already exists from LLMR (wave 1). This is 4 phases × 30 trials.
7. **Reproducibility settings are missing (finding 7).**
   - **Fix [W]:** add the temperature, maxTurns, both system prompts verbatim and the grader's normalization rules to an appendix.
   - **Fix [B, tiny]:** record them in `meta.json`.
8. **The false-alarm evidence is thin and is drawn from the same tasks.** It amounts to 37 clean calls.
   - **Fix [E, about 2 h, \$0, no LLM]:** sweep every v1 catalog entity and argument family through the proxy (`scenario v1-all` style) and report per-call and per-entity FP with Wilson bounds. Hundreds to thousands of clean calls are feasible. This is a lightweight version of the H1 frame (DOM2 and VOCAB are merged).
9. **The protocol section hides its own power analysis (finding 8).**
   - **Fix [W]:** state the MDEs and the PREREG admission that H3 and H4 have power below 0.5 at plausible effects.
   - **Fix [W]:** give a substantive justification for π0 = 0.02, or present it as a budget-driven choice.
10. **The detection counts are unexplained.** Of the 47 drift-phase calls, 41 are violated, 5 unverifiable and 1 verified.
    - **Fix [W]:** explain the 5 unverifiable and the 1 verified call, which are probably T3 and T6.

### Questions for authors
- Was decoding at the default temperature in every arm?
- Did the no-fiks arm see the system-prompt sentence about `fiks_receipt`?
- Which harness differences separate the 2026-10-18 run from the 2026-10-20 run?
- Was the "no answer = failure" rule fixed before the first run?

## R3: Novelty, positioning and presentation

**Scores:**
- Soundness 3/4: the claims are carefully hedged.
- Presentation 2/4
- Contribution 2/4
- Overall 4/10
- Confidence 3/5

### Summary
The paper is a careful, honest system and pilot report. Its novelty is a combination claim, and the evidence that the combination matters is deferred to an unexecuted study. The writing is precise sentence by sentence, but it is padded at the section level, repetitive, and shows its process.

### Strengths
- The "What is and is not new" paragraph and the loop-step matrix (Table 8) are unusually candid. Reviewers will appreciate the explicit concessions: receipts are not new, the vocabulary is standard data-quality checking, and AegisFlow, SapFix and Itzal are prior closed loops.
- The problem is timely and well motivated. Silent success-shaped failures at the MCP boundary, together with behavioural drift that leaves the spec unchanged, form a clear gap.
- Figure 1 tells the story of the loop.

### Weaknesses (ranked by severity)
1. **Contributions 3 and 4 are not contributions.** A "positioning against 508 sources" that the reader cannot see, and a pre-registration draft that is neither frozen nor reviewed, do not count. Neither does a subtitle advertising "a Pre-Registration Draft". Reviewers will read this as "the real paper isn't done".
   - **Fix [W]:** drop both from the contribution list and the subtitle. Describe the protocol as "planned evaluation" in one compact section (or an appendix), and link the catalog as a released artifact if it stays.
   - The defensible contributions are three: (i) the loop with its gate placement and gate-hardening evidence, (ii) the substrate (a drift layer in which the upstream changed and the tool did not, SYNTHESIS M1), and (iii) the pilot, including the receipt-wording negative result.
2. **The novelty is a combination, and nothing shows that the combination matters.** Every step exists in prior work (the authors' own Table 8). Without even one ablation (R1 W1), an SE or ML reviewer will call this integration engineering.
   - **Fix [E]:** run the R1 W1 ablation. It is the cheapest experiment that turns "we found no system that combines" into "the combination changes outcome X".
3. **The paper never shows its artifacts.** There is no generated repro test, no patch and no PR excerpt; the only artifact shown is a receipt.
   - **Fix [W]:** add one listing pair for D2, the repro test and the 3-line patch, and the PR body skeleton, perhaps as a "timeline of one drift" figure (violation → test → PR → merged → confirm). The CLAUDE.md dashboard spec already defines this timeline.
4. **§2 (Why this tool should exist) is long, redundant, and weakened by the Reddit thread.** §2 restates the introduction. Table 1 restates §2. The practitioner thread is unverified, self-selected, and includes a respondent who "described itself as an AI agent", so it costs more credibility than it adds.
   - **Fix [W]:** cut §2 to one paragraph plus Table 1, or merge it into the introduction. Remove the Reddit paragraph, but keep its scope list (what fiks does not address) in Limitations.
5. **There is a lot of repetition and process filler.**
   - "never merges" or "never merged" appears 5 times.
   - Scope and what is not validated are restated four times: the Scope paragraph, "What is validated here", §8 (Limitations) and the Conclusion.
   - "In the sources we reviewed (searched up to 2026-10-21)" appears twice.
   - Process sentences add nothing for the reader: "produced by competing designs, a judge panel and repeated adversarial critique", and "each citation checked against its source".
   - **Fix [W]:** state scope once, in the introduction, and the limits once, in §8. Keep the AI-use statement, and remove process narration elsewhere.
6. **The tables are overused or non-informative.** Table 5 has identical pass^k columns. Table 7 duplicates baseline rows. Table 1 duplicates prose. There is no results figure.
   - **Fix [W]:** use one per-task outcome figure (R2 W1) and one loop table (keep Table 4), and fold Table 7 into the figure as two extra rows.
7. **The bibliography has hygiene problems (finding 6).** It prints "[verify …]" markers, every entry is `@misc`, SapFix is cited via a blog, the bibliography is heavy on 2026 arXiv preprints and vendor blogs, and the capitalization is lowercased.
   - **Fix [W, about 1 h]:** fix the bib types, remove the markers, cite ICSE-SEIP SapFix, SOSP ClearView and τ-bench by venue, and mark vendor sources as "vendor documentation".
8. **The problem definition is too thin to support the design.** It does not define a contract formally, the semantics of verified, violated and unverifiable, or the observation assumption (the tap).
   - **Fix [W]:** add a half-column definition: a contract as a set of predicates over (x, r, e, probe), and verdict semantics, including when the verdict is unverifiable.
9. **The voice is inconsistent.** A single author writes "the authors" and "we wrote" throughout.
   - **Fix [W]:** use "I" or a consistent "we", and keep "the author is responsible" in the AI statement.
10. **The abstract leads with near-construction numbers.** "Flagged all 5, valid repro for each, one attempt each" follows from author-written drifts and contracts.
    - **Fix [W]:** lead with the problem and the gate design. Give feasibility numbers with the circularity caveat in the same sentence, and make the post hoc status of the receipt wording explicit in the abstract.

## Consolidated: top 3 likely rejection reasons per venue type

### arXiv-only (community reception; cs.SE or cs.AI)
1. **It reads as an AI-generated hackathon project dressed as a paper.** The signs are heavy hedging, repeated scope disclaimers, process narration, a Reddit thread as motivation, "[verify …]" markers in the references, and "pre-registration draft" in the title. *Mitigation:* the R3 W1, W4, W5 and W7 writing pass (about half a day).
2. **The evidence is a toy.** It rests on one mock vendor, five self-seeded drifts and 2–5-line patches; readers will say "of course it worked". *Mitigation:* show the artifacts, disclose the prompt hints, and add the cheap repair ablation plus the held-out oracle (R1 W1–W3).
3. **The key question, whether it beats a monitor plus a coding agent, is deferred.** *Mitigation:* a COMP-lite exploratory run labelled as such (about 45 repair runs).

(Logistics: arXiv endorsement is still open, per PROGRESS; that is not a quality issue.)

### SE workshop (for example an LLM4Code, APR, AIware or agentic-SE workshop, 4–6 pages)
1. **Length and focus:** 15 pages with half on motivation and protocol. *Mitigation:* cut to the loop, gate hardening, the pilot and one artifact listing.
2. **No baseline at all.** Workshops tolerate small n but not the absence of a comparison. *Mitigation:* R1 W1 (COMP-lite) or at least the hint-free prompt arm.
3. **Repair difficulty is trivial and the prompt leaks hints.** *Mitigation:* disclose (R1 W2) and add the 5-repetition hint-free runs.

At workshop level the paper is closest to acceptance: borderline accept after the writing pass, weak accept with COMP-lite.

### SE main track (ICSE, FSE, ASE)
1. **No comparative evaluation answering the RQs.** The tool-paper bar requires baselines, ablations and real subjects. *Mitigation:* the full confirmatory program (wave 2 ARMS, HOLD and RUN; wave 3 INTEG; runs R1–R11). This takes weeks.
2. **No real-world subjects.** There are no real silent-failure bugs in public MCP servers and no organic drift. *Mitigation:* the RB-R9, RB-R10, RB-R19 and RB-R20 streams (wave 2, unmerged) and the RBA analysis (wave 3).
3. **Construct and oracle validity.** Author-written drifts, contracts and tasks, no held-out oracle, known gate-passing fake fixes, and no human assessment of PR quality or acceptance. *Mitigation:* the BLIND and HOLD streams, the MUT-based laxity table, and a blind-review study (SYNTHESIS H26; needs human time).

### ML workshop (agents and tool use at NeurIPS, ICLR or ICML)
1. **The agent-outcome effect is not established:** n = 6 tasks, p = 0.50, the gain comes from one task, and run-to-run variation is half the gain. *Mitigation:* 25 or more tasks per SYNTHESIS G2, trivial baselines, and k = 10 (a few hours and about 1,000 short trials).
2. **One model family in every role.** *Mitigation:* add one open model to the bench (LLMR's local qwen path).
3. **The receipt v2 wording is post hoc and works as an upstream-value oracle.** *Mitigation:* a pre-specified receipt ablation (with versus without upstream values, a caution-prompt baseline) on held-out tasks.
